# Mini Info Books
Short standalone readers

## Mini-Book A – Why Factories Use Robots
Industrial robots excel at tasks that are dull, dirty, dangerous, or demand extreme consistency. They do not get tired, they maintain precision over thousands of cycles, and they free human workers for higher-skill work. The robots in your lab are scaled-down versions of the same technology used in automotive, electronics, food, and pharmaceutical plants worldwide.

## Mini-Book B – Anatomy of a 6-Axis Robot
A typical industrial robot has six rotating joints (axes).  
• Axes 1–3 move the arm through space (position).  
• Axes 4–6 orient the tool (wrist).  
Together they allow the tool to reach almost any position and orientation inside the work envelope. Understanding this separation of position and orientation is fundamental for every roboticist.

## Mini-Book C – From Unimate to Today
The first industrial robot (Unimate, 1961) was hydraulic and performed a single hazardous task. Within fifteen years, fully electric, microprocessor-controlled arms appeared. Today’s compact robots combine high speed, high repeatability, and flexible mounting. The two robots in RoboClaw Lab represent the modern end of that continuous development.
