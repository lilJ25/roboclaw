# ROBOCLAW Lab – Extended Factoid List
Quick, reliable facts for students and parents

**Industrial Robotics History**
• The first industrial robot, Unimate, began work at a General Motors plant in Trenton, New Jersey, in 1961.
• Unimate’s first job was extracting hot die-cast metal parts that were dangerous for human workers.
• George Devol filed the foundational patent for a programmable article transfer device in 1954.
• Joseph Engelberger is widely known as the “father of industrial robotics.”
• In 1973 KUKA introduced the FAMULUS — the world’s first industrial robot with six electromechanically driven axes.
• In 1974 ASEA (later ABB) launched the IRB 6, the first fully electric, microprocessor-controlled industrial robot.
• The global stock of industrial robots now exceeds three million units (IFR statistics).
• Most industrial robots still use the classic 6-axis articulated design introduced in the 1970s.

**Precision & Performance**
• Modern industrial robots can repeat a position with accuracy better than the thickness of a human hair.
• Pose repeatability of ±0.02 mm is common on high-performance compact arms.
• Cycle times measured in fractions of a second are routine in packaging and assembly cells.
• Industrial robots do not get tired and maintain consistency over thousands of cycles.

**KUKA KR 10 R1100-2**
• Maximum reach: 1101 mm
• Maximum payload: approximately 11 kg (rated payload often optimized near 5 kg for dynamics)
• Pose repeatability: ±0.02 mm
• Robot mass: approximately 55–56.5 kg
• Footprint: 208 mm × 208 mm
• Can be mounted on the floor, wall, ceiling, or at an angle
• Protection rating: IP65 / IP67
• Designed for high-speed packaging, assembly, machine tending, and inspection
• Part of KUKA’s Agilus family of compact high-performance robots

**ABB IRB 120**
• Reach: 580 mm
• Payload: 3 kg (up to 4 kg with vertical wrist)
• Robot mass: only 25 kg — one of the lightest multipurpose industrial robots
• Base footprint: 180 mm × 180 mm
• Pose repeatability: 0.01–0.02 mm range
• Can be mounted floor, wall, or ceiling
• Integrated signal and air lines to the wrist
• Frequently used in laboratories, electronics, and tight production cells
• Introduced as one of the smallest multipurpose industrial robots of its time

**Roboticist Skills & Mindset**
• Understanding axes, reach, and payload is foundational.
• Safety thinking is as important as programming skill.
• Real industrial robots move with purpose — every motion has a reason.
• Observation and clear documentation separate good programmers from average ones.
• The ability to isolate one variable at a time is a core troubleshooting skill.
• Most professional programs are refined many times before production use.

**Career Reality**
• Industrial roboticists work for system integrators, manufacturers, equipment makers, and end users.
• Common roles include programmer/integrator, application engineer, automation specialist, and maintenance technician.
• Hands-on experience with real industrial robots is highly valued by employers.
• Knowledge of one major brand (KUKA, ABB, Fanuc, etc.) is often a strong starting point.
• Safety standards (ISO 10218 and related) shape every professional installation.

**ROBOCLAW Lab Connection**
• The robots you control in ROBOCLAW Lab are the same class of machines used in real factories and laboratories.
• Direct physical experience with industrial arms is rare for most students and is a genuine advantage.
• Keeping a simple log of lab sessions builds both skill and a personal portfolio record.
